Knowing how to write a business letter is still a genuinely useful skill. Email handles the day-to-day, but for proposals, formal notices, references, and anything that needs to look official or go on the record, a proper business letter carries a weight that a quick message can't. This guide walks you through it step by step, with examples you can adapt, so your next professional letter reads as clear, credible, and polished.

## Step 1: Choose the Format

Most business letters use **block style**: everything left-aligned, single-spaced within paragraphs, with a blank line between each section. Set one-inch margins and pick a clean font — Calibri, Arial, or Times New Roman at 11–12 points. Block style is the safe default and the easiest to get right.

## Step 2: Add the Heading and Date

Start with your address (or your company letterhead), then the date, then the recipient's name, title, and address. For example:

> Jane Doe
> 12 Market Street
> Bristol, BS1 4RL
>
> 14 March 2026
>
> Mr. Thomas Reed
> Operations Director
> Reed & Co. Ltd

## Step 3: Open With a Proper Salutation

Address the recipient by name whenever you can: "Dear Mr. Reed," in the UK, or "Dear Mr. Reed:" with a colon in US business writing. If you don't have a name, use "Dear Sir or Madam," (UK) or "To Whom It May Concern," (US) — but a quick search to find the right name almost always pays off.

## Step 4: State Your Purpose in the First Line

Busy readers scan. Open with the reason for writing: "I am writing to propose…", "I am writing to confirm…", or "I am writing to request…". No throat-clearing — get to the point in sentence one.

## Step 5: Write a Clear, Concise Body

Keep the body to two to four short paragraphs, one idea each. Give the reader what they need — dates, figures, reference numbers — and cut everything else. Stay formal: avoid slang, and skip contractions ("do not" rather than "don't") to keep the tone professional.

## Step 6: End With a Call to Action

Close by telling the reader what happens next: "I would welcome the chance to discuss this — please call me on…", or "I look forward to your confirmation by 30 April." A clear next step makes the letter easy to act on.

## Step 7: Sign Off Correctly

Match the closing to your salutation. UK convention: "Yours sincerely," when you named the recipient, "Yours faithfully," when you didn't. US convention: "Sincerely," or "Best regards." Leave space for a signature, then type your name and title beneath it.

## A Quick Example

> Dear Mr. Reed,
>
> I am writing to propose a three-month pilot of our delivery-tracking service for Reed & Co.'s Bristol depot.
>
> Over the past year we have reduced missed deliveries by 22% for comparable clients. I have enclosed a one-page summary of how the pilot would work and what it would cost.
>
> I would welcome the chance to discuss this. Please call me on 0117 496 0000 at your convenience.
>
> Yours sincerely,
>
> Jane Doe
> Account Manager

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the correct format for a business letter?

Block style is the standard: left-aligned text, single spacing within paragraphs, a blank line between sections, one-inch margins, and a clean 11–12pt font. It includes a heading, date, recipient's address, salutation, body, closing, and signature.

### How do I start a business letter?

Open with a formal salutation ("Dear Mr./Ms. [Surname],") and then state your purpose in the very first sentence, so the reader immediately knows why you're writing.

### How do I end a business letter?

Finish with a clear call to action, then a sign-off that matches your greeting: "Yours sincerely" or "Yours faithfully" in the UK, "Sincerely" or "Best regards" in the US. Leave room for your signature and print your name and title below.

### Should a business letter be handwritten or typed?

Typed and printed, in almost all cases — it's clearer and more professional. A handwritten signature above your typed name is the usual finishing touch.
