If you want to learn how to write a letter that actually lands, the good news is that the structure barely changes from one occasion to the next. Whether you're thanking a friend, catching up with family, or sending a formal note to a bank or landlord, the same building blocks apply: a heading, a greeting, a clear body, and a fitting sign-off. Get those right and everything in between falls into place. Here's a step-by-step guide you can follow every time you sit down to write.

## Step 1: Choose the Right Format

Before you write a word, decide whether the letter is personal or formal, because that choice shapes everything else.

- A **personal letter** can start straight in with a warm greeting and a chatty tone.
- A **formal letter** follows a set layout: your address (top right in the UK, top left is also common in the US), the date, the recipient's name and address, then the greeting.

If you're printing on your own stationery, a single sheet of A4 (UK) or Letter-size paper (US) with one-inch margins keeps things clean and professional.

## Step 2: Open With the Right Greeting

The greeting sets the tone in a single line.

- **Personal:** "Dear Sarah," "Hi Tom," or even "My dear James," depending on how close you are.
- **Formal, name known:** "Dear Mr. Clarke," or "Dear Ms. Rivera,".
- **Formal, name unknown:** "Dear Sir or Madam," (UK) or "To Whom It May Concern," (US).

Always follow a name you know with a comma; in US business letters a colon is common ("Dear Ms. Rivera:").

## Step 3: State Your Purpose Early

Don't bury the reason for writing. In the first paragraph, tell the reader why you're getting in touch: to thank them, to ask something, to update them, or to lodge a complaint. A reader who knows the point straight away stays with you to the end.

## Step 4: Write a Clear, Focused Body

Keep each paragraph to one idea and move logically from one to the next. Concrete details do the heavy lifting: instead of "thanks for everything," write "thank you for driving two hours to help me move the sofa." For a formal letter, stay courteous and avoid slang and contractions. If you're not sure where the line sits, our guide to [letter writing etiquette](/en/blog/letter-writing-etiquette) covers the dos and don'ts in detail.

## Step 5: Close With the Right Sign-Off

Match your closing to your greeting and your relationship:

- **Personal:** "With love," "Warmly," "All the best,".
- **US formal:** "Sincerely," or "Best regards,".
- **UK formal:** "Yours sincerely," when you opened with a name, "Yours faithfully," when you didn't.

Leave space for a signature, then print your name beneath it.

## Step 6: Proofread Before You Send

Read the letter once for meaning and once purely for spelling, grammar, and punctuation. Reading it aloud catches clumsy sentences your eye glides over. If the letter matters, set it aside for an hour and return with fresh eyes. For a longer note that needs a personal touch, our tips on [how to personalize a letter](/en/blog/the-power-of-personalized-letters) will help you make it feel like it could only have come from you.

## A Quick Letter-Writing Checklist

- Correct format for the occasion (personal vs. formal)
- Greeting that matches your relationship
- Purpose stated in the opening paragraph
- One idea per paragraph, concrete details throughout
- Sign-off that pairs with your greeting
- Proofread for spelling, grammar, and clarity

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I start a letter?

Open with a greeting that fits the relationship: "Dear [Name]," for someone you know, "Dear Mr./Ms. [Surname]," for a formal contact, or "Dear Sir or Madam," when you don't have a name. Then state your reason for writing in the first line or two.

### What's the difference between a personal and a formal letter?

A personal letter is relaxed in tone and needs little more than a greeting and a friendly close. A formal letter follows a fixed layout — addresses, date, formal salutation, and a matching sign-off like "Yours sincerely" or "Sincerely."

### How do I end a letter?

Choose a sign-off that mirrors your opening. In the US, "Sincerely" or "Best regards" work for most formal letters; in the UK, use "Yours sincerely" when you named the recipient and "Yours faithfully" when you didn't. For personal letters, "With love" or "Warmly" suit the tone.

### How long should a letter be?

Long enough to make your point and no longer. Most personal letters run a page; formal letters are often shorter, ideally staying to a single side so the reader can take it in at a glance.

## Start Writing Better Letters Today

Writing a good letter isn't about fancy language — it's about a clear structure and a genuine message. Follow these steps, keep the reader in mind, and your next letter will read exactly the way you meant it.
