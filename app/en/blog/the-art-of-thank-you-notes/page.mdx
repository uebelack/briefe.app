Staring at a blank thank you card, unsure what to write, is one of life's small recurring stresses. The trick is that a thank you note doesn't need to be long or clever — it needs to be specific and sincere. In fact, there's a simple formula that etiquette writers have used for generations, and once you know it you'll never be stuck for words again. Here's what to write in a thank you note, line by line, with wording ideas and examples.

## The Four-Line Thank You Note Formula

A short, well-judged note usually has four moves:

1. **Greet and thank them for the specific thing.** "Dear Ben, thank you so much for the beautiful scarf."
2. **Say something about it.** "The color is perfect, and it's already become my go-to for chilly mornings."
3. **Add a personal or forward-looking line.** "It was so kind of you to think of me — I hope we can catch up soon."
4. **Close warmly.** "Thank you again. Warmly, Ruth."

Four sentences. That's a complete, heartfelt thank you note.

## Wording Ideas by Occasion

**For a gift**
"Thank you for the [gift] — it was such a thoughtful choice, and I'll think of you every time I use it."

**For hospitality**
"Thank you for having us for dinner. The food was wonderful, but it was the company that made the evening."

**For a kind gesture**
"Thank you for [what they did]. It made a real difference at exactly the right moment, and I'm so grateful."

**For a wedding or big event**
"Thank you so much for celebrating with us and for your generous gift. It means the world to have had you there."

## The One Rule That Matters Most

Be specific. "Thanks for the gift" could go to anyone; "thank you for the hand-painted mug — it's on my desk and it makes me smile every morning" could only go to one person. Naming the gift and saying one true thing about it is the difference between a note that feels dutiful and one that feels warm.

## A Few Points of Etiquette

- **Send it promptly** — within a week or two is ideal, but a late note is always better than none.
- **Handwrite it when you can** — a handwritten card carries more warmth for personal occasions, though a neatly printed note is perfectly acceptable and easier to read.
- **Keep it positive** — even if the gift wasn't quite to your taste, thank the giver for their thoughtfulness sincerely.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What do I actually write in a thank you note?

Follow the four-line formula: thank them for the specific thing, say something nice about it, add a personal or forward-looking line, and close warmly. Four sincere sentences are plenty.

### How is a thank you note different from a thank you letter?

A note is short and casual — usually a card — and perfect for gifts and social occasions. A letter is longer and more structured, better suited to interviews or formal gratitude. For the longer version, see our guide on [how to write a thank you letter](/en/blog/tips-for-writing-a-thank-you-letter).

### Is it too late to send a thank you note?

Almost never. Aim for within a week or two, but if more time has passed, send it anyway with a brief, honest acknowledgment. People are far happier to receive a late note than none at all.

### Should thank you notes be handwritten?

For personal occasions, handwriting adds warmth and is well worth the effort. That said, a cleanly printed note is completely appropriate — especially if your handwriting is hard to read or you're sending several at once.
