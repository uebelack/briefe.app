The standard business letter format is built from seven parts, and once you know them the whole thing becomes a template you can reuse forever. Whether you're writing to a client, a supplier, or a public office, following the accepted structure signals that you know what you're doing. This guide breaks the format down part by part, in the order they appear on the page, with a full block-style example at the end.

## 1. The Heading (Your Address and Date)

Start with your return address — or, if you have one, your company letterhead — followed by the date. In block style everything sits flush left. Write the date in full to avoid confusion between US and UK conventions: "14 March 2026" (UK) or "March 14, 2026" (US).

## 2. The Inside Address

Below the date, add the recipient's details: their full name, their title, the company, and the postal address. Getting the title and spelling right matters more than people think — it's the first sign of care the reader sees.

## 3. The Salutation

Greet the recipient formally. "Dear Mr. Reed," or "Dear Ms. Rivera," is standard; US business writing often uses a colon ("Dear Ms. Rivera:") where UK writing uses a comma. When you have no name, "Dear Sir or Madam," (UK) or "To Whom It May Concern," (US) will do — though a named greeting is always stronger.

## 4. The Body

This is the substance. Keep it to two to four paragraphs, each covering a single point:

- **Opening paragraph:** state your purpose in the first sentence.
- **Middle paragraph(s):** give the detail — the facts, figures, and context the reader needs.
- **Closing paragraph:** summarize and point to the next step.

Stay formal and precise: no slang, and avoid contractions to keep the tone professional.

## 5. The Complimentary Close

Sign off in a way that matches your salutation. UK: "Yours sincerely," (name known) or "Yours faithfully," (name unknown). US: "Sincerely," or "Best regards." Capitalize only the first word and follow it with a comma.

## 6. The Signature

Leave three or four blank lines for a handwritten signature, then type your full name and job title beneath it. The signature is what turns a printed sheet into a formal, personal document.

## 7. Enclosures and Extras

If you've attached anything, note it below your name with "Enclosure" or "Enc." When the letter is going to someone other than the addressee too, add "cc:" and their name. These small conventions keep everything unambiguous.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the seven parts of a business letter?

The heading (your address and the date), the inside address, the salutation, the body, the complimentary close, the signature, and any enclosures or extras. Together they make up the standard block-style format.

### What is block style?

Block style is the most common business letter layout: every line is aligned to the left margin, paragraphs are single-spaced with a blank line between them, and there are no indents. It's clean, professional, and hard to get wrong.

### Where does the date go in a business letter?

Directly below your return address (or letterhead) and above the recipient's inside address. Write it out in full to avoid US/UK ambiguity.
